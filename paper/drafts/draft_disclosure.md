# Draft: AI-assistance disclosure (to be adapted to the journal's exact policy wording)

Springer Nature journals require that the use of generative AI tools in
preparing a manuscript be disclosed in the Methods or Acknowledgements,
that AI tools are not listed as authors, and that the authors take full
responsibility for the content. A draft statement, to be adjusted once the
journal is named and its policy page read:

**Use of AI tools.** Large language models were used in two distinct roles
in this work. (1) As the object of study: the generators evaluated in
Sections 3 to 6 were written by the DeepSeek model named in Section 4.3,
under the prompts released in the artifact; this is the experiment. (2) As
an assistant during the research: an AI coding assistant (Claude, Anthropic)
was used for writing and refactoring the evaluation and analysis code, for
running and monitoring the experiment batches, for drafting text that the
authors then revised, and for checking numbers against the generated
reports. All experimental design decisions, the pre-registration, the
interpretation of results, and the final text are the authors' own, and the
authors have verified every number in the paper against the committed data.
No AI tool is an author of this work.

Note to the user: the second sentence of (2) is the honest description of
this project's working history and is what the journal's policy asks for.
It cannot be omitted without the statement becoming false; see the
standing note on disclosure in the project's memory.
